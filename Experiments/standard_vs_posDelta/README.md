# Standard Attention vs Positional ΔW

This folder contains the experiment comparing a standard Transformer attention
model with a **PosDelta** variant that adds a structured, fixed positional
perturbation to the Query and Key projections.

The purpose of this experiment is to isolate one question:

> **Can attention benefit from an explicit positional modification of its
> Q/K projections, without using a HyperNetwork to generate the modification?**

This is the **positional-change experiment** in the larger project.

---

## 1. Models Compared

### Standard

The Standard model uses:

- `d_model = 128`
- `2` attention heads
- `3` attention hops
- `d_ff = 256`
- standard scaled dot-product attention
- a `[CLS]` token for classification
- normal learned Q/K/V/O projection matrices

The attention operation is

$$
Q = XW_Q,\qquad
K = XW_K,\qquad
V = XW_V
$$

followed by

$$
A =
\operatorname{softmax}
\left(
\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_h}} + M
\right).
$$

The final prediction is obtained from the `[CLS]` representation.

---

### PosDelta

The PosDelta model keeps the same main Transformer dimensions but introduces a
structured positional correction to Q and K.

Instead of using only

$$
Q = XW_Q,\qquad K = XW_K,
$$

the model uses

$$
Q' = Q + \alpha_Q X\Delta W_Q
$$

and

$$
K' = K + \alpha_K X\Delta W_K.
$$

Here:

- \(\Delta W_Q\) and \(\Delta W_K\) are fixed positional perturbations.
- \(\alpha_Q\) and \(\alpha_K\) are learnable scalar parameters.
- The positional perturbations are constructed from sinusoidal positional
  features.
- The perturbations use a **rank-4 block-structured construction**.

The resulting attention is therefore

$$
A =
\operatorname{softmax}
\left(
\frac{Q'K'^T}{\sqrt{d_h}} + M
\right).
$$

The important point is that **the positional ΔW itself is not dynamically
generated by a HyperNetwork**. The structure is fixed; only its magnitude is
learned through the scalar Q/K gates.

---

## 2. Pooling Difference

The two models also differ in how the final sequence representation is formed.

### Standard

The Standard model uses a special `[CLS]` token:

$$
z = x_{\mathrm{CLS}}.
$$

### PosDelta

PosDelta does not use `[CLS]`. It uses a masked mean over the real tokens:

$$
z =
\frac{\sum_t m_t x_t}
{\sum_t m_t},
$$

where \(m_t=1\) for real tokens and \(m_t=0\) for padding.

Thus the experiment changes both:

1. the attention mechanism through positional ΔW, and
2. the final sequence pooling mechanism.

---

## 3. Experimental Setup

The models are evaluated on six bAbI tasks:

| Task | Reasoning setting |
|---|---|
| `bAbI-qa1` | Single supporting fact |
| `bAbI-qa2` | Two supporting facts |
| `bAbI-qa3` | Three supporting facts |
| `bAbI-qa15` | Basic deduction |
| `bAbI-qa16` | Basic induction |
| `bAbI-qa19` | Path finding |

Each model is trained using five random seeds:

```text
42, 43, 44, 45, 46
```

The reported result is:

$$
\text{mean accuracy} \pm \text{standard deviation}.
$$

---

# 4. Final Results

| Task | Standard | PosDelta | Δ (PosDelta − Standard) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `bAbI-qa1` | **55.60 ± 2.82%** | 52.70 ± 1.36% | **−2.90 pp** |
| `bAbI-qa2` | 34.96 ± 0.56% | **35.80 ± 0.36%** | **+0.84 pp** |
| `bAbI-qa3` | **22.16 ± 1.38%** | 21.40 ± 0.92% | **−0.76 pp** |
| `bAbI-qa15` | **71.06 ± 5.85%** | 61.22 ± 0.93% | **−9.84 pp** |
| `bAbI-qa16` | 49.62 ± 0.55% | **49.96 ± 0.59%** | **+0.34 pp** |
| `bAbI-qa19` | 13.44 ± 2.17% | **21.90 ± 5.49%** | **+8.46 pp** |

---

# 5. Interpretation

### bAbI-qa1

PosDelta decreases performance by:

$$
-2.90
$$

percentage points.

The positional perturbation does not appear beneficial for the single-fact
task.

### bAbI-qa2

PosDelta gives a small improvement:

$$
+0.84
$$

percentage points.

The two models remain relatively close.

### bAbI-qa3

PosDelta is slightly worse:

$$
-0.76
$$

percentage points.

This is a small difference compared with the variability across runs.

### bAbI-qa15

This is the largest negative effect.

PosDelta drops by:

$$
-9.84
$$

percentage points.

The Standard model reaches:

$$
71.06\%
$$

while PosDelta reaches:

$$
61.22\%.
$$

### bAbI-qa16

The models are essentially tied:

$$
49.62\% \quad \text{vs} \quad 49.96\%.
$$

The difference is only:

$$
+0.34
$$

percentage points.

### bAbI-qa19

This is the strongest positive result.

PosDelta improves from:

$$
13.44\%
$$

to

$$
21.90\%,
$$

an increase of:

$$
\boxed{+8.46\text{ percentage points}}.
$$

---

# 6. Main Conclusion

The PosDelta experiment does **not** show a universal improvement over standard
attention.

Instead, its effect is strongly task-dependent.

The largest positive effect occurs on:

$$
\boxed{\text{bAbI-qa19 (Path Finding)}}
$$

while the largest negative effect occurs on:

$$
\boxed{\text{bAbI-qa15 (Basic Deduction)}}.
$$

Therefore the current result can be summarized as:

> **A structured positional modification of Q/K can be useful for some
> reasoning problems, but adding positional ΔW does not consistently improve
> Transformer reasoning performance.**

This experiment is useful because it provides the **positional baseline** for
the next experiment, where the weight modification itself becomes dynamic and
input-dependent through a HyperNetwork.
